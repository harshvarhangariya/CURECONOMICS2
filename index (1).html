Growth is rarely a straight line.

Tumours, economies and product demand often grow in an S-shape: slowly at first, then fast, then flattening near a limit. Cureconomics fits two classic S-curves to your data and compares them.

Lab 1: derivative and integral

The calculus behind the curves. Differentiation gives the growth rate; integration rebuilds the curve from that rate.

d/dt
dN/dt = rN(1 − N/K)

The derivative is the instant growth rate. It is largest when N is about half of K, which is where the S-curve is steepest.

∫
N(t) = ∫ (dN/dt) dt

Integrating the rate over time gives the cumulative size. Solving this differential equation produces the Logistic formula.

Two models, one comparison.

K is the upper limit, r the growth rate, A the starting shape and t is time.

Logistic growth

N(t) = K / (1 + A·e^(−rt))

Symmetric around its fastest-growth point. Growth slows as N nears K.

Gompertz growth

N(t) = K·e^(−A·e^(−rt))

Asymmetric: it grows quickly early and approaches K more slowly.
